# Fretboard Trainer

The Fretboard Trainer shows scales, arpeggios and chords on a guitar neck, plays them, and gives you exercises and quizzes to learn them. Open it with **Fretboard** in the session header. Anything you play or practise in it starts the **Open Session** timer, and what you worked on (key, scale, position, tempo, quiz kind…) is written into the Open Session's notes.

## Getting started

- **The pages.** Use the tabs at the top: Scales, Arpeggios, Chords, Progressions, Practice, Speed and Quiz. The key buttons (C, Db, D…) change the key on every page.
- **Hearing things.** Press **Play** (or the space bar) to hear what's on the neck. Click any note on the neck to hear just that note. If you hear nothing, see *Playback and sound* below.
- **Light and dark.** In Settings > Appearance > Theme, pick Dark, Light or the same as the computer.
- **Keyboard shortcuts.** Space: play / stop. Left and Right arrows: previous / next position or shape. F: focus mode. Esc: leave focus mode (or close a window). F1: this help.

## The neck

Every note is a circle coloured by its scale degree; the root has a white ring.

- **Colours and labels.** Each degree family has its own colour (root, 2nd, 3rd…); flat or sharp degrees use a deeper shade. Labels can show the degree (1, b3, 5), the note name, both, the interval (R, m3, P5), or nothing, and the colours can be Muted, Nordic, "Root, 3rd & 5th only" or Vivid — both in Settings > Appearance.
- **Positions.** Pick a position at the top (or use the arrow keys). The position is highlighted and its frets can get extra room; everything else is dimmed and the scale's other notes show faintly. All three can be changed in Settings > The neck.
- **Show degrees and strings.** In Settings > The neck, click a degree chip to hide or show that degree (for example only roots and 5ths), or a string chip to hide that string's notes.
- **Show previous / next.** Shows the whole position below and/or above yours, a little faded, so you can see how they join.
- **Connect to.** Join your position to another one along the strings you pick (for example only the A string). Play then climbs your position, travels along that string, and finishes in the other position.
- **High E on top.** In Settings > The neck: turn it off to turn the neck over if you prefer the low string at the top.
- **Tuning.** In Settings > The neck: standard, half-step down, Drop D, DADGAD and Open G. Everything (positions, shapes, quizzes) follows the tuning.

## Scales

Over 50 scales, grouped by sound: major-sounding modes, minor-sounding modes, pentatonic & blues, harmonic and melodic minor families, exotic, bebop and symmetric scales.

- **Tonality line.** Under the title: whether the scale sounds major or minor, and how it differs from the plain major or natural minor scale (for example Dorian = natural minor with a natural 6).
- **About card and colour notes.** The card on the right says how the scale sounds, when to use it and songs that use it. Its colour notes — what makes it sound like itself — are listed and ringed in gold on the neck.
- **Blend with.** Lay a second scale over the first. Notes in both look normal; notes only in the first get a teal ring; notes only in the second are diamonds.
- **Chords in this key.** On the right: the chords built from the scale. Click to hear one, double-click to open it on the Chords page.
- **Play over a progression.** Plays a progression with drums behind the scale. With **Suit the scale** on (the default) it picks one that fits: the scale's own preset if it has one, otherwise one built from the scale's chords, otherwise a common one for its sound. Turn it off to use the progression from the Progressions page. The scale stays exactly as it is; the notes of the chord playing light up with a coloured ring and a badge showing their role.
- **Playing.** Play runs through the position up, down, or up and down (Direction), at the tempo and notes-per-beat you choose.
- **Drone.** While a scale plays on its own, a soft drone holds the scale's root chord underneath (root, 5th and the scale's own 3rd — E Phrygian gets an E minor drone; scales with no 3rd get a root-and-5th). Turn it on or off next to the tools; its volume is in Settings > Sound. It also plays under ladder and sequence practice and Speed trainer scale runs.
- **Positions past the 12th fret.** The position list carries on up the neck: after the first set come the same shapes an octave up (or down), so you can play every position all the way to the 22nd fret.

## Arpeggios

The notes of a chord across the neck, in positions starting on each chord tone.

- **Chord type.** Triads, 6ths and 7ths, extensions (9, 11, 13) and altered dominants.
- **Play over a progression.** The arpeggio switches to each chord of the progression as it plays, staying in your position.

## Chords

Playable chord shapes with the root in the bass on the 6th, 5th and 4th strings.

- **Shapes.** Step through shapes with the Shape menu or arrow keys. The chord box on the right shows the fingering; muted strings are marked with an X.
- **Strum and Play.** Strum plays the shape once; Play strums it and then picks through it.
- **Play over a progression.** Each chord of the progression is shown as it plays, near the shape you chose.

## Progressions

Loop chord progressions with strumming and drums, and see the chord tones on the neck.

- **Presets.** Pop, jazz ii–V–I, 12-bar blues, minor, modal vamps and more. Choosing one also picks a scale that suits it.
- **Editing.** Click a chord card to hear and edit it: root, chord type, how many beats it lasts, move it left or right, duplicate or remove it (removing asks first). Add chords from the key or any chord from the panel below.
- **Saving.** Name the progression and Save; it appears under My progressions. Deleting a saved one asks first.
- **Guitar and drums.** Choose how the guitar plays (one strum, every beat, fingerpicking, bass + strum) and a drum style (rock, pop, ballad, half-time, funk, shuffle, bossa, jazz) with its own volume.
- **Follow the chords.** Off keeps the key's scale; "Scale for each chord" switches to the right scale for each chord (D Dorian over Dm7); "Arpeggio of each chord" shows just the chord. Both stay in your position.
- **Highlight.** "Chord shape played" makes the strummed shape biggest; "All chord tones" highlights every chord tone in the position.

## Practice

Exercises that play along and show each step on the neck. Pick a routine, set it up, press Play.

- **Position ladder.** Every position of the scale, low to high: up one, down the next (or up and down each).
- **CAGED.** The five CAGED shapes of the key's major or minor chord, low to high. For each shape: strum the chord, play its arpeggio up and down, then the scale around the same shape (at most three notes per string). The scale is the one chosen at the top when it holds the chord, otherwise the major or natural minor scale. Needs standard tuning.
- **Chord changes in one position.** Each chord of your progression as an arpeggio or its scale, without leaving your position, optionally with the chord strummed.
- **Extended arpeggios.** Stacked thirds past the octave — 7th, 9th, 11th, 13th — on every chord of the key or just the I chord.
- **Scale sequences.** Your position in thirds, groups of 3 or groups of 4.
- **Circle of fifths.** Your scale in all 12 keys, up in fifths (C G D A…) or down in fourths (C F Bb Eb…), staying in your position. The next key is shown, and can be spoken, two beats ahead.
- **Chord relay.** Every chord of the key in order, each in the next shape up the neck on one set of strings, then back down. Triads or 7ths, strummed or one note at a time.
- **Time keeping.** Just the click, for practising your own time.
- **Click patterns.** Every beat, beats 2 and 4, beat 1 only, bars on and off, or random dropped beats — so you keep the time yourself.
- **Speed bursts.** Each run plays once at the normal speed, then again in short groups of notes at double speed, each followed by a rest as long as the group. A badge by the step's name shows which pass is playing. Shared with the Speed trainer's setting.
- **Call-outs and voice.** Show each note's name, interval or degree as it plays; **Speak call-outs** says them out loud. Voice speed is in Settings > Sound.
- **Speed trainer (tempo-raising).** Raises the tempo by a set amount every few loops, up to a limit.
- **Step list.** The chips under the options are the steps of the routine. Click one to see and hear it.

## Speed trainer

Build speed on a run you choose: the tempo climbs on its own in timed rounds, with rests in between, and every session is logged.

- **Setup.** Pick what to play — a scale run, an arpeggio, a scale sequence or the progression's arpeggios — then the session length (5–30 minutes), the start and target tempo, how long each round lasts and how long the rests are. Speed bursts (off, 3, 4, 6 or 8 notes) add a second pass to every run: once at the round's tempo, then again in short groups at twice the tempo, each followed by a rest as long as the burst, so you touch tempos you can't hold yet. While playing, a badge shows which pass you're in: "Normal speed" or "SPEED BURSTS". The line under the settings shows the plan, for example "7 rounds of 60 s with 20 s rests: 80 to 120 bpm".
- **Playing.** Press **Start session**. Each round plays the run in a loop with the metronome; at the end of the round there's a rest with a countdown, and the next round starts a step faster. The last round is at your target tempo. The strip shows the whole session and where you are.
- **Linked to the rest of the app.** The run uses the Key, Scale and Position at the top, the chord type for arpeggios, and the progression from the Progressions page — the same ones you practise elsewhere.
- **Progress.** Every session is saved with the start tempo, the highest tempo reached and the time spent — also if you stop early. The chart shows the trend across sessions, for all runs or one kind.

## Quiz

Timed quizzes; your answers and times are saved for a report.

- **Kinds.** Name the note, find the scale degree, name the interval, hear the interval, and hear the chord quality.
- **Start and timing.** Press Start; each answer is timed. Stop ends the session and shows a summary.
- **Answer out loud.** Tick "Answer out loud (microphone)" and, once a session is running, just say the answer: a note ("C sharp", "B flat", "G"), a degree ("flat three", "five", "root" — or the note's name), an interval ("minor third", "perfect fifth", "tritone") or a chord ("major", "minor seven", "diminished"). Say "next" to move on, "hear it" (or "again") to hear the question again, and "stop" to end the session. While answering out loud, the next question follows three seconds after each answer, until you say "stop". Your time is measured to when you started speaking. A small meter shows the microphone; it stops listening while the app's own guitar is sounding, so it doesn't answer itself. It uses macOS's own speech recognition, on this Mac when the Mac supports it (otherwise through Apple's service — hover the status to see). The first time, macOS asks whether Practice Hub may use the microphone and speech recognition. If something is blocked, the message says where to allow it, with a Try again button; the trainer itself keeps running either way.
- **Both note names.** Notes that have two names show both, like C#/Db and A#/Bb, in the answers, the explanations and the weak spots. Degree answers keep the spelling that belongs to the key (the 4th of Bb is Eb), and the explanation adds the other name.
- **Keyboard answers.** Each answer has a key, shown in its corner: 1 to 9, then 0, Q and W for answers 10 to 12 (in order, left to right, top to bottom). After answering, Enter or Space goes to the next question.
- **Saved between sessions.** Every answer and its time is saved on your computer as you go, so your history and the report are still there after you close and reopen the app. The score and streak at the top are for the current session.
- **Weak spots everywhere.** What you get wrong or answer slowly in the quizzes follows you round the app: on the Scales, Arpeggios, Chords, Progressions and Practice pages those notes get a small ! badge (hover one to see your score), and the side panel lists your weak spots in the current key — for example the 4th in Bb — with a "Quiz me on these" button. In the Quiz, "Focus on my weak spots" asks more about them. A weak spot is worked out from your last 6 tries at it, and clears after 3 right answers in a row at your usual pace or faster. Your usual pace is your typical (middle) time for that kind of question over your last 5 sessions, plus 30% — so it keeps up as you get faster and one distracted answer doesn't move it. It uses however many of those sessions you have; until you've answered 10 questions of a kind there's no fair yardstick yet, so speed isn't judged — only right and wrong count — and Settings shows how many answers are still needed — or a fixed time you set in Settings > Quiz and weak spots. Degrees count per key (the 4th of Bb is separate from the 4th of F); note names count everywhere (every Eb, in any key). Turn the marks off in Settings > The neck.
- **Report.** Open the report to see accuracy, average and fastest times, recent sessions, and the notes or intervals you're slowest on or miss most. Clearing the history asks first.
- **Frets.** Limit questions to frets 0–5, 0–12 or the whole neck.

## Playback and sound

The bar at the bottom controls playback and sound on every page.

- **Tempo, notes per beat, direction, loop.** Set the speed in bpm, how many notes per beat (quarters, eighths, triplets, sixteenths), the direction for scales and arpeggios, and whether playback loops.
- **Count-in.** Every playback starts with a count-in (1 bar by default; Off or 2 bars in Settings > Playback). It plays once, not on every loop.
- **Guitar sound.** In Settings > Sound (or click the sound's name on the bottom bar): real guitar samples from the bundled SoundFont (steel, nylon, jazz, clean, overdrive and more) or the built-in synthesized guitars. Use **+ Add SoundFont** to add .sf2 files you download; the settings link to a page of free guitar SoundFonts. Removing one asks first.
- **Tone and room.** In Settings > Sound: tone (warm to bright), room reverb, and fingers or pick for the built-in guitars. Volume stays on the bottom bar.
- **Output and Test.** In Settings > Sound, choose which speakers or headphones to use (where the webview allows it) and press Test to hear a short strum. If the output stops, the trainer says so.

## Focus mode

Press **Focus** (or F) to hide everything except the neck, the progression and the play controls. Esc leaves it.

## Your files

Saved progressions, quiz history, the speed log, settings and added SoundFonts are kept in this app's own storage on your computer.
